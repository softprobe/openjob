One employer, 60 identical titles

OpenJob · November 20, 2026

Board

The caveat up front: this is a small, fun finding from our November 18, 2026 analysis of a 5,107-row sample of the OpenJob index (shards 0, 7, 14, 20, 27, 33; both SHA-256 checksums verified against the manifest). It is a quirk in the sample, not a claim about any employer beyond it.

The most common job title in the sample is "Sales Manager_Chinese Vertical". It appears 60 times. Every single one of the 60 is from the same employer: Chowbus.

Sixty rows is 1.2% of the whole sample, from one title at one company. And it distorts the category it sits in: 207 titles in the sample contain the word "sales" (4.1%), and 60 of them — nearly 3 in 10 — are this one Chowbus title. Read the raw ranking naively and "Sales Manager_Chinese Vertical" looks like the hottest job in the index. Read it properly and it is one employer's hiring pattern, repeated.

Not a duplicate problem

This is not a deduplication failure. The sample has 0 duplicate indexed_ids — 5,106 distinct IDs across 5,106 indexed rows (the 5,107th row is the one native post, Softprobe's own role). The 60 Chowbus rows are 60 distinct rows. The title repeats; the rows do not. The underscore-joined title reads like a field straight out of an ATS export, and the employer presumably posts the same role across many teams, cities, or verticals — but the dataset does not say which, and we are not going to guess. That is the point: the data can tell you the title repeats 60 times, and it cannot tell you why.

Why title counts need normalizing

Any "most common job title" ranking built on raw counts will crown whichever employer posts the most copies of one title. The fix is boring and it works: count distinct employers per title alongside raw counts, or cap each employer's contribution to a title before ranking. The honest headline is never "the most common title is X" — it is "the most common title is X, posted by N employers". For this one, N is 1.

That is also why the role rankings in the roles article count keywords across titles rather than ranking exact titles: keywords survive one employer's repetition better than exact strings do. Even there, the same caution applies — a keyword ranking is only as broad as the employer base behind it.

How we counted

  • Source: the public manifest at https://openjob.softprobe.ai/api/data/manifest.json, generated 2026-11-18T20:08:03Z; 33,107 jobs, 34 shards, complete: true. Today's manifest lists 33,362 rows; the counts here are from the November 18 snapshot.
  • Sample: 5,107 rows from shards 0, 7, 14, 20, 27, 33. The shards are source-blocked, not random — each shard holds exactly one ATS system — so this is a cluster sample. The sample has no Lever rows (Lever is 6.0% of the full dataset) and overweights Ashby.
  • Indexed ≠ recruited. Every number describes rows indexed from employer ATS boards. OpenJob did not recruit these employers; the employers own the text.
