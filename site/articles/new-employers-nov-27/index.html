Eight more big names joined the board in one evening — four of them show the salary

OpenJob · November 28, 2026

Board

I maintain OpenJob — and the board just had its biggest single day of growth, for the third evening in a row.

As of this morning, November 28, 2026, the board lists 49 companies and 49 roles. Yesterday it listed 41 of each. The jump came in one evening batch on November 27: eight companies and their roles were added to the board in pull requests #188 through #203 — Grafana Labs, GitLab, Datadog, Docker, Redis, Temporal, Airbyte, and PostHog.

That is a 20% increase in a day — seven companies on November 25, eight more on November 26, eight more again on November 27 — and the names this time are the ones every engineer already has on their resume shortlist. Observability, DevOps, data infrastructure, and the tools that run the modern stack: this batch rounds out the board's coverage of the layer below the AI companies, and half of them put the pay in the listing.

  • Airbyte — Staff Backend Engineer, Agent Infrastructure — no salary stated

Two details are worth noting. Docker's top end, $260,700, is the highest salary ceiling posted on the board so far. And PostHog does something the other seven don't: it publishes a salary calculator anyone can open, so the pay is transparent even though no fixed range is filed — transparency in a different form, and the role page links straight to it. Six of the eight roles are senior or staff level: Grafana Labs, Datadog, Docker, and Redis are all hiring Senior engineers, and Temporal and Airbyte are both hiring Staff engineers.

Every page links to the employer's own listing

Each company page on the board links to the employer's own careers page, and each job page links to the employer's own application page. Docker is hiring a Senior Software Engineer for AI Developer Tools; Datadog is hiring a Senior Software Engineer; GitLab is hiring a Fullstack Engineer for Source Code: Repository Services; Temporal is hiring a Staff Software Engineer for AI Foundations (Agent Optimization).

The point is simple: the board does not intermediate the application. A job seeker reads the role on OpenJob and applies where the employer takes applications. Nothing about the listing is reworded to look bigger than it is, and the employer's own page is always one click away.

What "on the board" means, honestly

Here is the boundary, stated plainly. These company and job pages were added to the board by the maintainer, from the employers' public ATS listings. The employers have not signed up, posted themselves, or endorsed the board — the pages say where each role is listed and link to the employer's own application page. The one role posted natively by its own employer is Softprobe's own: Software Engineer — AI Agent QA & Observability, posted November 13.

That is why the board is built the way it is. A company page is reviewed once when it is created, and after that only its owners can edit it — so any of these 49 employers can claim their page, correct it, or close a role at any time by opening a pull request. Board listings carry a 60-day expiry; company pages never expire. The board grows by adding pages; the employers stay in control of what their pages say.

The board and the index, side by side

The native board is the small, curated half of OpenJob. The other half is the indexed dataset: the manifest generated 2026-11-28T15:37:40Z lists 33,246 rows pulled from employer ATS boards. That number is not a count of live jobs — the honest live figure, from our November 23 recompute, is about 14,000 roles posted in the last 60 days (42.1% fresh in a 5,438-row sample; about 13,650 on the ATS-weighted estimate). The full method is in How we count and We counted our own index honestly.

The two halves do different jobs. The index is the searchable haystack — tens of thousands of roles your agent can search. The board is the curated shelf: 49 companies, 49 roles, each with a page, each linking to the employer's own listing, each expiring after 60 days unless its owner renews it. The shelf grows one verified page at a time — seven in one evening, then eight, then eight again — and that is the growth worth watching.
