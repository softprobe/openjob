OpenJob

A job board with no tracking, on purpose

OpenJob · November 20, 2026

Board

OpenJob's front page says it in one line: free for everyone, no account, no tracking. That is not a marketing claim. It is a description of how the board is built — and, where it matters, it is enforced by the same checker that validates every post.

No tracking, because there is nothing to track with

Searching the board needs no account, no sign-in, and no cookie banner to dismiss. The pages load their scripts from OpenJob itself — the board's own app.js, share.js, i18n.js, and a vendored QR-code script — and fetch their data from OpenJob's own index. There are no third-party scripts, no analytics beacons, and no ads. A job board for agents has no use for knowing who you are: your agent does the matching, the board just publishes the facts, and the facts do not improve if we watch you read them.

Posting is the one place an account exists, and it is not ours. A job is a pull request on GitHub, so the login is GitHub's. OpenJob holds no passwords and no credentials of its own; there is nothing here to leak.

No phone numbers, anywhere, ever

The post checker rejects a phone number in any post — job, talent profile, or company page. It also rejects the disguises: name [at] domain, fullwidth characters, HTML entities, %40, +86 mobiles, and Chinese numerals. A phone number is the one contact method that cannot be filtered, unsubscribed from, or revoked, so the board does not allow it in any form.

No emails on profiles — and one address, at most, on a job

A talent profile cannot contain an email address at all. The contact for a person is the GitHub pull request that published the profile, plus an optional personal website or LinkedIn link. No WeChat ID, no WhatsApp handle, no Telegram handle either.

A job may carry exactly one hiring address, and only on the company's own domain — mailto:jobs@example.com when email is the only application method, or as apply_email alongside an https:// application link. A personal address, or any address on another domain, is rejected. The address lives in the front matter only: it is never copied into the body, and it is never copied into share text. A company page carries no hiring address at all — the hiring address belongs on the job.

That last rule is the point. A hiring address on a job is a fact a candidate needs. The same address in a share card is exposure the employer never asked for. The board prints it where it is useful and withholds it where it is not.

Expiry is privacy, too

Data that does not linger cannot leak later. A job post lasts 60 days from its posted date; a talent profile lasts 180 days unless renewed. Expiry is enforced by the board, not requested of the poster — a scheduled workflow removes expired files from the live tree. A talent profile also carries a private delete token, so its owner can take it down at any time; the honest caveat is that deletion cannot recall copies someone already downloaded. Posts are public by design, under CC BY 4.0, and public means public.

Why this is a design decision, not a feature

A conventional job board collects contact details because its business is the details: resumes to sell, candidates to message, behavior to measure. OpenJob's business is a public file format. The board earns trust by holding less, not by guarding more — every field it refuses to collect is a breach that cannot happen and a consent dialog that never appears. The checker is the mechanism: the privacy rules are not a page that asks you to believe us, they are a test that every post has to pass before it goes live.

The rules are written out in full in CONTRIBUTING.md under "What the check enforces" and "Privacy", at github.com/softprobe/openjob. If a rule is missing there, it is not a rule — and if a post breaks one, the checker, not a moderator's mood, says so.
