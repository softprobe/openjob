Each pipe has a different shape

The pipes are not interchangeable. Moka is the Chinese-language pipe: 97.8% of Moka titles in the sample contain CJK characters, and Moka supplies the largest employers in the Chinese half of the index — DJI (425 rows), 远景能源 (312), 新浪微博 (220), 阶跃星辰 (220). Greenhouse is the English-language pipe with the biggest names: Cloudflare (493), Stripe (222), Robinhood (166), Scale AI (166), Reddit (130). Ashby is the smallest pipe and the freshest — 67.2% of Ashby rows were posted in the last 60 days, against 43.4% for Greenhouse and 33.4% for Moka — and the only one that ever shows pay: every salary string in the sample comes from an Ashby board.

Lever is the blind spot. It is 6.0% of the full dataset — 2,000 rows — and we know nothing about it from the sample, because the six shards we downloaded contain no Lever shard. Any claim about the whole index that leans on the sample is really a claim about the other three pipes. The weekly re-run should add a Lever shard.

Why publish the plumbing?

Because the pipes explain the numbers. The index is stale where Moka is stale and fresh where Ashby is fresh; it is bilingual because Moka is Chinese-language; it shows pay only where Ashby shows pay. A dataset that hides its sources invites its users to mistake a source artifact for a market fact. Naming the four pipes — and the one we have not measured — is the difference.
