Eight big names joined the board in one evening — six of them show the salary

OpenJob · November 27, 2026

Board

I maintain OpenJob — and the board just had its biggest single day of growth, for the second evening in a row.

As of this morning, November 27, 2026, the board lists 41 companies and 41 roles. Yesterday it listed 33 of each. The jump came in one evening batch on November 26: eight companies and their roles were added to the board in pull requests #170 through #186 — MongoDB, Supabase, Vercel, Stripe, Databricks, Sentry, Cockroach Labs, and Cloudflare.

That is a 24% increase in a day — seven companies on November 25, eight more on November 26 — and the names this time are the ones job seekers already know. These are not only startups: the batch brings the board its first household-name infrastructure employers, and most of them put the pay in the listing.

The salary ranges below are as posted on the employers' own pages on November 26:

  • Stripe — Staff Software Engineer, Code Forge — $224–336K
  • Vercel — Software Engineer, Platform — $208–312K (San Francisco)
  • Cockroach Labs — Member of Technical Staff (Storage) — $180–238.1K
  • Databricks — Staff Software Engineer, Fullstack — CAD $173,400–$238,350

  • Cloudflare — Senior Software Engineer — no salary stated

Two details are worth noting. Databricks posts its range in Canadian dollars — the first non-USD salary filed on the board. And six of the eight roles are senior or staff level: MongoDB's SRE role is Senior or Staff, Stripe, Databricks, and Sentry are all hiring Staff engineers, Cockroach Labs is hiring a Member of Technical Staff, and Cloudflare's role is Senior. The board is not only growing — it is growing at the level experienced engineers actually apply for.

Every page links to the employer's own listing

Each company page on the board links to the employer's own careers page, and each job page links to the employer's own application page. Stripe is hiring a Staff Software Engineer for Code Forge; Sentry is hiring a Staff Software Engineer for Platform & Infrastructure; MongoDB is hiring a Site Reliability Engineer for its Storage Layer Services; Cloudflare is hiring a Senior Software Engineer on its Developer Productivity team.

The point is simple: the board does not intermediate the application. A job seeker reads the role on OpenJob and applies where the employer takes applications. Nothing about the listing is reworded to look bigger than it is, and the employer's own page is always one click away.

What "on the board" means, honestly

Here is the boundary, stated plainly. These company and job pages were added to the board by the maintainer, from the employers' public ATS listings. The employers have not signed up, posted themselves, or endorsed the board — the pages say where each role is listed and link to the employer's own application page. The one role posted natively by its own employer is Softprobe's own: Software Engineer — AI Agent QA & Observability, posted November 13.

That is why the board is built the way it is. A company page is reviewed once when it is created, and after that only its owners can edit it — so any of these 41 employers can claim their page, correct it, or close a role at any time by opening a pull request. Board listings carry a 60-day expiry; company pages never expire. The board grows by adding pages; the employers stay in control of what their pages say.

The board and the index, side by side

The native board is the small, curated half of OpenJob. The other half is the indexed dataset: the manifest generated 2026-11-27T16:46:40Z lists 33,235 rows pulled from employer ATS boards. That number is not a count of live jobs — the honest live figure, from our November 23 recompute, is about 14,000 roles posted in the last 60 days (42.1% fresh in a 5,438-row sample; about 13,650 on the ATS-weighted estimate). The full method is in How we count and We counted our own index honestly.

The two halves do different jobs. The index is the searchable haystack — tens of thousands of roles your agent can search. The board is the curated shelf: 41 companies, 41 roles, each with a page, each linking to the employer's own listing, each expiring after 60 days unless its owner renews it. The shelf grows one verified page at a time — seven in one evening, then eight the next — and that is the growth worth watching.
